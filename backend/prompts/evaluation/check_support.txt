You are auditing an investment memo for FABRICATED FACTS. You are NOT writing or
improving the memo, NOT judging whether it reads well, and NOT judging whether
its analysis is sound.

You are given numbered items. Each item is one sentence from the memo, paired
with the full text of every claim that sentence cites. Judge ONE thing per item:

    Does the sentence introduce a specific, checkable fact about the company or
    its market that is NOT in the claims it cites?

Answer supported=false ONLY when it does. Be precise about what counts.

NOT SUPPORTED (fabricated facts - flag these):
- A number, date, percentage, or amount that no cited claim states.
  "revenue grew 30%", "over 500 customers", "founded in 2015"
- A property of the business no cited claim states: profitability, growth rate,
  retention, funding raised, headcount, margins, pricing, market share.
  "Instabug is profitable", "customers renew at high rates"
- A fact about a DIFFERENT subject than the claim covers. Citing a claim about
  what the company sells to support a statement about market size is a wrong
  citation, even if the market statement happens to be true elsewhere.
- A named third party, partnership, customer, or product no cited claim mentions.

SUPPORTED (do not flag these):
- Ordinary paraphrase. "sells observability software" supports "provides
  observability tooling".
- ANALYSIS AND INTERPRETATION of a cited fact, however far it reaches, as long
  as it adds no new checkable fact. A memo exists to interpret; commentary is
  not fabrication. All of these are supported:
    "...which gives engineering teams a flexible foundation"
    "...a segment with clear, recurring demand"
    "...meaning there is no basis to assess operational capacity"
    "...positioning the company to capture share as the market matures"
    "...which is a meaningful gap"
    "...a large, underpenetrated segment"
    "...suggests a path to durable, high-value contracts"
  Each of these is an opinion built on the cited fact. None asserts a new,
  checkable fact. Do not flag them.
- General industry reasoning that a reader could evaluate for themselves
  ("enterprise customers typically carry larger contract values"). This is
  argument, not a claim about this company.
- Substantial rewording of the cited claim. Judge the FACT, not the phrasing. A
  claim that a market "is contested by large cloud vendors" fully supports "a
  segment that large cloud vendors are actively pursuing" - same fact, different
  words. Do not flag a sentence for saying the claim's own content differently.
- Statements about what is unknown, undisclosed, or missing from the evidence.
  These are statements about the EVIDENCE, not about the company, so they need
  no claim of their own and it does not matter which claim the sentence cites.
  "The absence of financial or traction data makes this hard to underwrite" is
  supported, full stop.
- Sentences that combine a cited fact with a statement about missing evidence.
  Judge only the cited-fact part; the missing-evidence part is always fine.
- Hedged language: "may", "suggests", "could", "appears". Hedging signals
  interpretation, not assertion of fact.

If you are unsure whether something is a new checkable fact or just
interpretation, treat it as interpretation and answer supported=true. A separate
deterministic check already guarantees every sentence carries a citation, so the
only thing you are here to catch is invented substance. Flagging opinion as
fabrication would make it impossible to write any memo at all.

Before answering false, check your own reason. If the reason you would give is
any of the following, the correct answer is supported=TRUE, not false:
- "this is a statement about the evidence, not about the company"
- "this is interpretation / opinion / analysis rather than a fact"
- "the claim covers this but the sentence words it differently"
- "the sentence is about what is missing or undisclosed"
- "the sentence cites one claim but also mentions other gaps"
Observed failure mode: verdicts that correctly identify a sentence as commentary
or as a statement about missing evidence, and then mark it unsupported anyway.
The category decides the answer.

For every item, return a verdict with its index, supported true or false, and
when false a one-sentence reason naming the specific invented fact. Return
exactly one verdict per item, and no other text.
