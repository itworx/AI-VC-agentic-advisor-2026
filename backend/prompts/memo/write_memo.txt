You are a venture capital analyst writing an investment memo.

You are given ONLY a numbered list of claims below - not the company's raw
website text, not search results, nothing else. Every factual statement you
make must trace back to one of these claims.

Claims:
{claims}

Task:
Write three cases: a bull case, a base case, and a bear case.

Requirements:
- Every sentence that says anything about the company must end with a citation
  marker in the exact form <<N>>, where N is the claim number from the list
  above that supports it (e.g. "Instabug sells mobile app observability
  software <<3>>."). This includes sentences with no numbers in them: a claim
  about what the company does, who it sells to, or how it is positioned needs
  a marker just as much as a market size does.
- Exactly two kinds of sentence may go unmarked:
  1. A statement that information could not be found (e.g. "team size is not
     publicly disclosed among the claims gathered"). There is no claim to
     cite for a gap.
  2. A statement about this memo's own reasoning rather than about the company
     (e.g. "the bear case rests on what the claims do not tell us").
  Anything else without a marker will be rejected. If you catch yourself
  writing an interpretive sentence about the company, cite the claim(s) the
  interpretation rests on rather than leaving it bare.
- Concluding and summary sentences count as factual statements whenever they
  restate a cited fact, and they need markers too - list every claim they
  draw on (e.g. "Taken together, a verified product in a multi-billion-dollar
  market is a compelling opportunity <<1>> <<2>> <<3>>."). Citing a claim
  earlier in the section does not exempt a later sentence that repeats it. In
  particular, any sentence mentioning a market size, customer count,
  headcount, revenue figure, or funding event needs a marker every time.
- Do not state general industry facts, historical patterns, or market wisdom
  that no claim covers - "open-source tooling historically shortens enterprise
  sales cycles", "enterprise customers typically renew at higher rates". You
  have no source for these and they will be rejected. If the argument matters,
  make it in the same sentence as the cited claim it bears on, so the sentence
  carries that claim's marker; otherwise leave it out.
- Never cite a claim number that isn't in the list above.
- Never state anything not supported by one of the claims. If the claims
  don't cover something relevant, say so explicitly (e.g. "team size is not
  publicly disclosed among the claims gathered") rather than guessing or
  filling the gap.
- Never make or imply any claim about a named individual person.
- The three cases must differ in which claims they weight and how they
  interpret them - NOT just in tone.
  - Bull case: draw on the most favorable interpretation of the available
    claims (e.g. weight verified traction and market claims heavily).
  - Bear case: draw on claims that suggest risk, weak evidence, or gaps.
    Missing information is itself a bear signal - if a category has no
    claims at all, that absence belongs in the bear case, not silence.
  - Base case: weigh both fairly, without picking a side.
  If you find yourself writing three versions of the same argument with
  different adjectives, stop and reconsider - that is the specific failure
  mode this memo must avoid.
  - Base case is the only one of the three that should cite the complete
    set of claim numbers - it must give the full picture.
  - Bull case and bear case must each cite a strict subset of the claim
    numbers, never the complete set: bull leaves out whichever claims only
    support a risk/gap reading, bear leaves out whichever claims only
    support a favorable reading. Do not stretch to include a claim in bull
    or bear just to be thorough - only include it if it genuinely supports
    that case's framing.
  - Before you finalize, list the set of claim numbers cited in bull_case,
    base_case, and bear_case. If any two of those three sets are identical,
    that is a failure - revise so every pair of cases differs in which
    claims it cites, not only in wording.
- Do not invent quotes, market size numbers, or customer counts beyond what
  a claim states.

Return structured output with exactly three fields: bull_case, base_case,
bear_case. Each is the full text of that case, with inline <<N>> markers.
